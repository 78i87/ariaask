# Cognitive load (mental effort as a signal)

Cognitive load is mental effort. Two complementary rules govern it:

1. **All effective learning involves high cognitive load — but not all high load is
   effective.** Learning happens when the brain processes and organizes information,
   and that processing is felt as effort. Standing on one foot juggling while reading a
   textbook maxes out load without producing learning; the load must come from
   *processing the material* (connecting, organizing, comparing, generating), not from
   ambient difficulty.
2. **Low load is a red flag.** If a study activity feels mentally easy — boring,
   monotonous, autopilot, drowsy — it is almost certainly passive and producing little
   learning. Rereading and word-for-word note rewriting are the classic cases.

## The passive-learning audit

Track a study session or two and notice which methods put the brain in that passive
state. Then simply delete them and study a topic without them. Most learners find their
results don't drop at all — revealing that a large fraction (often the majority) of
their study time was waste. Reinvest that time in high-load techniques: nonlinear
relational notes, teaching a concept simply, creating and critiquing analogies, free
recall.

## Overload is the other failure mode

Load must be high but *within capacity*. Working memory is severely limited (see the
memory-handling doc); exceeding it is cognitive overload — felt as stuck, confused,
"reading the same paragraph four times." Under overload, no useful output happens.
The responses are: pause intake and process what's already on the workbench, narrow to
two or three elements at a time, offload onto paper, and build context first so new
pieces weigh less (schemas make items lighter — familiar structures chunk many pieces
into one).

**The target zone:** effortful but not overloaded — the same zone as desirable
difficulty (see encoding-vs-review). Comfortable = too passive; drowning = overloaded.
Both are signals to change the process, not signs of inability.

## Coaching cues

- "Studying feels easy and I retain nothing" → load audit; delete passive methods.
- "I'm working so hard and nothing sticks" → check whether effort is going into
  processing the material or into fighting overload/distraction; fix intake rate first.
- Learner proud of long passive hours → reframe: effort-per-learning, not hours.
