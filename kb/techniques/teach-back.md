# Teach-back (learning by teaching)

**What it's for:** The deepest all-in-one workout in the toolbox. Teaching something
forces nearly every high-value process at once: **free retrieval** (recalling without
cues), **simplification** (explaining to a novice — Sung's "teach it to a 10-year-old"),
**organization** (a lesson needs a coherent structure, which means grouping and
sequencing), and **gap exposure** (the moment you can't explain a step simply, you've
found precisely what you don't understand). Sung's own workshop party trick — learning
an audience member's topic live and teaching it back from memory — is this technique
running on top of strong encoding. Reading or preparing *with the intention to teach*
also measurably improves processing quality, even before any teaching happens.

**When:** After initial encoding of a topic, when understanding feels "probably fine" —
teach-back is the fastest way to find out it isn't. Excellent for consolidation before
exams, for verifying a new professional domain, and whenever a learner says "I
understand it, I just can't explain it" (which means they don't yet).

**How:**
1. **Prepare as if teaching**, not as if reviewing: decide what matters most (judging),
   in what order, with what examples and analogies.
2. **Teach it** — to a study partner, a rubber duck, a doc, or an AI student — from
   memory, closed book. Simplify ruthlessly; jargon is hiding places for gaps.
3. **Field questions.** Questions are where teach-back beats solo summarizing: a
   learner's probing "but why?" forces retrieval down paths you didn't rehearse and
   exposes the difference between knowing the words and knowing the structure.
4. **Capture the gaps.** Every stumble, hand-wave, or "it just is" goes on a list —
   these are precise, high-value gaps. Re-encode them (connect, simplify, map) and
   teach that part again.

**In this app:** This is exactly what the built-in teach-back mode is — an AI student
(Aria) who learns only what the user teaches, responds with calibrated confusion, and
asks probing questions. When a learner needs deep understanding of a topic they've
already had first exposure to, recommending "press *Teach it back* and teach Aria" is
recommending this technique with a tireless, always-available student.

**Common mistakes:** Teaching with notes open (kills the retrieval); lecturing in the
source's jargon and order (transcription with extra steps — restructure it); skipping
questions; explaining *around* a gap instead of writing it down and fixing it.

**Signs it's working:** Explanations get shorter and simpler over iterations; questions
stop being scary; the gap list shrinks; the "wow, I never thought of it that simply"
reaction from listeners.
