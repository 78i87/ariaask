# Beating procrastination (the addiction model)

**What it's for:** Everyone procrastinates; a procrastination **addiction** is
different — dependency plus withdrawal. High-frequency, low-effort dopamine spikes
(feeds, shorts, autoplay) condition the brain to expect a reward every ~30 seconds and
to need more of it just to feel normal. Nothing meaningful in life pays out on a
30-second timer, so an addicted reward system quietly closes the door on every long
project — and it damages focus and self-regulation broadly (mimicking ADHD symptoms,
which is a different, neurodevelopmental thing — but the overlap confuses many people).

**The self-test (simple, unpleasant):**
1. Build a **distraction cheat sheet**: attempt focused work with paper beside you;
   log every distraction, as specifically as possible.
2. Remove *all* of them completely.
3. Watch what happens: mind wandering within minutes, actively seeking substitute
   distractions (downloading junk games you don't even enjoy just to have *something*),
   and restlessness/discomfort when you resist. Seeking + withdrawal = addiction.

**The protocol:**
1. **Cold turkey, not weaning.** For serious cases, gradual reduction rarely works; cut
   to zero (you can reintroduce moderated use later, and withdrawal from this can't
   hurt you — it's just uncomfortable).
2. **Remove triggers.** The app icon itself is a trigger (reflexively reopening an app
   you closed seconds ago). Delete apps or use blockers; phone in another room, do not
   disturb, out of sight.
3. **Dopamine detox.** A stretch of days genuinely cut off — nature works well — lets
   the reward system recalibrate; the mind noticeably settles. Design it as something
   to look forward to, not to dread. Alone it won't hold: re-exposure reactivates the
   loop, hence step 4.
4. **Mindfulness meditation** — the same focus-muscle training as in focus-training:
   thoughts and urges arise, you practice *not pursuing them* and letting them drift
   past. 20–30 minutes daily for 2–3+ weeks before benefits appear. This is the
   long-term relapse protection.

**Common mistakes:** Judging yourself lazy or dumb (it's conditioning, and it's
reversible — even after a decade); weaning gradually while keeping every trigger in
reach; a weekend detox with no meditation follow-through; treating meditation's
distracted moments as failure (they're the reps).

**Signs it's working:** Sitting with nothing no longer itches; starting work stops
requiring a countdown; the phone loses its gravitational pull; focus sessions lengthen.
