# Priming (zoom out before details)

**What it's for:** Building the context (schema) that makes new information click.
Confusion from new material usually isn't about the material itself — it's a missing
surrounding schema. "They finally did it" is meaningless until you know who "they" are;
rereading the same sentence adds nothing because all the understanding lives in the
context around it. Experts learn faster within their domain precisely because they have
more anchor points to connect to.

**When:** Before diving into details of any new topic, lecture, paper, or chapter —
especially when you already feel confused. If you don't understand something, do NOT
just go over it again: repeated passes with the same process keep missing the same
thing. Zoom out instead.

**How:**
1. **Map the big picture first.** Skim headings, titles, and structure; deliberately map
   out the topic's main concepts so you can see the gap the new information is meant to
   fill. It's much easier to connect a schema you can see.
2. **Ask priming questions while consuming.** Three questions that force connection:
   *Why is this important? How can I use or apply this? What is the main point in its
   simplest form?* Actively asking these primes the brain to notice and grab exactly
   that type of information.
3. **If still confused, ask the fourth question:** *What would I need to already know
   for this to become easier?* — then go get that prerequisite first.
4. **Fast context builders:** have AI generate a simplified layperson's summary of the
   topic (big picture in plain words, minimal terminology), or put key terms into Google
   Images to find flow diagrams and charts — the brain processes visual structure far
   faster than prose, and a good diagram *is* a visible schema.

**Common mistakes:** Using AI summaries as a replacement for processing rather than as
context for it (the summary primes; the learning still requires your own encoding).
Skipping priming because it "isn't real studying" — it multiplies the speed of
everything after it.

**Signs it's working:** New details start slotting in on first read; less rereading;
you can predict where the material is going.
