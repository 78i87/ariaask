# Analogy creation & critique

**What it's for:** Forcing deep comparison between new material and prior knowledge.
An analogy highlights how two different things are similar ("life is like a journey").
Creating one requires holding both concepts up and comparing structures — exactly the
high-cognitive-load processing that produces durable learning. It's also one of the
fastest ways to hook abstract material onto things you already understand.

**When:** Any conceptual material, especially abstract or mechanism-heavy topics;
whenever a learner says "I sort of get it but couldn't explain it."

**How:**
1. **Create:** After learning a concept, deliberately build an analogy to something
   familiar. Ask: what is this *like*? What plays the same role?
2. **Critique — this is where the value multiplies:** Examine your analogy for accuracy
   and comprehensiveness. Where does it break down? "Life is a journey — but journeys
   have known destinations and life doesn't… so life is a *spontaneous* journey — but
   journeys rarely drop you somewhere you never intended… so it's a spontaneous journey
   through a foggy, treacherous forest." Each refinement forces re-examining the actual
   material more deeply and critically.
3. Iterate until the analogy carries the important structure of the concept, and note
   explicitly what it still gets wrong (the residue is often the concept's most
   distinctive part).

**Evidence check you can run yourself:** Take two topics you just studied. Make a quick
analogy for one and stop; make an analogy for the other and spend time critiquing and
polishing it. A week later, the critiqued topic will almost always be sharper — in
memory, depth, and nuance.

**Common mistakes:** Making an analogy and never critiquing it (most of the learning is
in the critique); accepting an analogy that's vivid but structurally wrong; borrowing
someone else's analogy — the processing only counts if your brain does the comparing.

**Signs it's working:** It feels effortful; the analogy evolves through versions; you
can say precisely where it breaks down.
