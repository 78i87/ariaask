# Higher-order learning (Bloom's & SOLO, aim high early)

Deep processing and strong encoding happen at the **higher orders of learning**. Someone
who has encoded at a high level has clear organizational structure: they know not just
what things are but how everything fits, can navigate the topic logically from any entry
point, and can explain it so simply it sounds obvious.

## The ladder (Bloom's revised taxonomy)

- **Remember** — recall isolated facts and details.
- **Understand** — explain a concept. Still isolated! "Understanding" a single concept
  deeply is *not* yet real learning; isolation is the problem.
- **Apply** — solve concept-specific problems.
- **Analyze** — the first genuinely relational level: bring multiple ideas together,
  compare and contrast them, see the relationships between them.
- **Evaluate** — know the *nature and importance* of those relationships: which matters
  most, how they combine (A leads to B; A+B produce C), how it all sits in the big
  picture.

SOLO taxonomy says the same thing from another angle: learning progresses from isolated
ideas → relational structure → extended big-picture significance. Both agree: **relate
information to other information and to the big picture, always.**

## Aim high as early as possible

The counterintuitive move: don't work up the ladder floor by floor. Set your objective
at analyze/evaluate from the start — the brain automatically fills in remembering,
understanding, and applying on the way there. You can't evaluate what you haven't
understood, and that's precisely why aiming at evaluation forces the understanding to
happen — with the big picture in view, so the information organizes itself correctly
the first time. Consuming pieces in isolation first ("memorize, then understand, then
someday connect") starts the organization on the wrong foot and has to be unlearned
later. Retention from first-pass relational encoding can reach 80–90% weeks later,
versus the ~50% one-week baseline.

If full relational thinking is overwhelming at first, step down one level: ask "how can
I apply this?" of everything, then level up to comparing and evaluating as tolerance
grows.

## Test and study at the level you'll be used at

Exams (and real life) at university level and beyond examine analysis and evaluation —
dissecting a complex question, choosing relevant concepts, connecting them. Studying and
self-testing at the remember/understand level while being examined at analyze/evaluate
is the classic mismatch that makes "the complicated questions" the ones that hurt (see
retrieval-practice).

## Coaching cues

- "I understand each part but can't do the exam questions" → they're at
  understand-level, examined at analyze/evaluate; move to relational techniques
  (mapping, deep processing loop) and higher-level self-testing.
- "Where do I start, the basics?" → start with the big-picture goal and let the basics
  fill in en route (with priming for context).
- A learner explaining with simple, obvious-sounding clarity → higher-order encoding
  achieved; raise the challenge.
