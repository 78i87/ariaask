# Mind mapping (GRINDE)

**What it's for:** The flagship encoding technique for conceptual material. The perfect
mind map is **not the artifact — it's the process**: copying someone else's map
transfers nothing, because the learning is the thinking that produced it. Mapping forces
grouping, comparison, connection, and judgment — the deep processing loop externalized
onto paper.

**When:** Conceptual (the "C" in PACER) material: theories, systems, relationships,
anything that lives in a network. Also planning essays and literature reviews.

**How — the six GRINDE checks:**

1. **G — Grouped.** Arrange related ideas into categories. The value is in *deciding*
   the grouping (by what similarity? there are always multiple options, and the
   textbook's isn't automatically yours) — research calls the payoff chunking/schema
   formation. Both the deciding and the resulting structure strengthen memory.
2. **R — Relational.** Express how ideas relate. Too few relationships = isolated facts;
   *too many* = unreadable spaghetti. Consciously choose which relationships matter
   enough to draw, and know each one's nature (cause/effect, sequence, influence…).
3. **I — Interconnected.** Connect the groups to each other into one overall structure.
   The failure pattern is **islands**: dense clusters with weak links between them —
   compartmentalized knowledge that can't be used fluidly for complex problems. Zoom
   out: can you see how the whole topic hangs together?
4. **N — Nonverbal.** Drop the word count; use lines, arrows, and spatial arrangement
   instead of sentences (the generation effect). Optionally add small abstract sketches
   as "memory landmarks." Wordy maps mean the processing didn't happen.
5. **D — Directional.** Give the map flow with purposeful arrows. Direction forces you
   to pin down the nature of each relationship and makes the map dramatically more
   organized and memorable.
6. **E — Emphasized.** Make judgments: visually mark the most important groups and
   relationships — the map's **backbone**. Emphasizing often reveals your grouping was
   wrong, forcing a restructure. That's not wasted time; the back-and-forth is the
   "recursive nature of deep learning," and being able to say *what matters most and
   why* is the hallmark of expertise.

**Skill levels (self-diagnosis):**
- **Level 0** — linear, wordy, superficial notes with highlighting: uselessly
  time-consuming.
- **Level 1** — nonlinear with lines and arrows, but messy, sentence-by-sentence
  transcription with connections bolted on. Necessary training wheels; no real benefit
  yet — most people quit here and conclude "mind mapping doesn't work."
- **Level 2** — groups, flow, deliberate structure; you think 20–30+ seconds before
  writing anything. Worth roughly a 10–15% grade bump over level 0.
- **Level 3** — quality: clear backbone, deliberate emphasis, *intuitive* groups
  (relabeled in your own terms), alternatives considered and judged. Rare; top-10%
  performance territory. Level 0→2 matters less than 2→3.

A top learner can produce level-3 *thinking* even in linear notes (read long, synthesize
mentally, write a mini-essay of the relationships) — but it's 2–3× slower and harder
than mapping, because the map tracks your thoughts for you. Synthesis-essays work better
as a revision technique.

**Using AI with mapping:** Harmful if it (a) does the organizing for you or (b) bypasses
your comparing and judging — e.g. "generate groups for these keywords" defeats the whole
point. Helpful if it saves collection time (gathering, summarizing raw material *for you
to then process*) or verifies a hypothesis you already formed ("do these groups I made
make sense?") — that's just a faster Google check.

**Common mistakes:** Copying a map (or having AI make one); starting from "central
bubble + branches" instead of letting the map emerge from grouping; drawing every
relationship you can find; islands; skipping E; expecting mastery in two attempts —
levels take weeks of deliberate practice, and each GRINDE step is best trained roughly
one at a time.

**Signs it's working:** Maps get simpler while understanding gets deeper; you can
review a topic by glancing at the backbone; disconnected stragglers on the map predict
exactly what you'll forget (gap-finding without testing).
