# The ladder method (effort-based passes)

**What it's for:** Studying effectively when tired, burnt out, or dreading a big topic —
and beating the procrastination that comes from overwhelm. The brain constantly judges
how much energy a task will need (it consumes ~20% of resting energy and aggressively
avoids waste); "I don't want to study" is that judgment. The three energy sinks while
learning are understanding what's coming in, comparing it to what you know, and deciding
where it belongs — doing all three at full depth simultaneously is what overwhelms.

**When:** Any large/dense topic; days when energy is low; starting anything that
triggers "there's so much here."

**How:** Split the topic into **rungs of effort**, not sections:
1. **Rung 1:** Go through the *whole* topic touching only what feels low-effort right
   now — the parts that are easy to understand, easy to compare with what you know, and
   easy to place. Skim or outright skip everything that feels hard. Track thoughts with
   nonlinear notes. This builds a scaffold in ~15 minutes and is a legitimate stopping
   point on an exhausted day.
2. **Rung 2:** Same rule — take what now feels low-effort. Because the scaffold exists
   ("the cups are set up"), material that was hard on rung 1 is easy now. This rung goes
   deeper and takes somewhat longer, with effort shifting from rough understanding to
   refining organization and checking connections.
3. **Rung 3:** Usually finishes the topic — remaining details go into the map or into
   flashcards depending on information type (see PACER).

Each rung costs about the same (deliberately low) effort; the ladder splits the total
work so no session is hard. It compounds: the more structure exists, the cheaper the
next pass. The same principle works for projects and assignments — easy high-level
planning first, progressively more detail per pass.

**Common mistakes:** Grinding through hard parts on the first pass ("I can't move on
until I understand this") — that's the overwhelm generator the ladder exists to avoid;
treating rung 1's skipping as cheating; doing rungs section-by-section instead of
whole-topic-per-rung.

**Signs it's working:** Starting stops being the hard part; sessions end before
exhaustion; the topic feels noticeably easier on each return.
