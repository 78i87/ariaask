# Interleaving (multiple angles, not just mixed schedules)

**What it's for:** Making knowledge usable from any direction. Testing yourself only
the way you learned it produces narrow knowledge: fine on the flashcard-shaped
question, lost on the curveball, the concept combination, the slightly out-of-scope
ask — exactly the questions that separate top learners from the rest.

**What it actually is:** People reduce interleaving to a schedule trick ("30 min maths,
30 min science, repeat" instead of blocks). The research contrast is with *massed
practice* (one thing, one block, one way), but the potent ingredient is **varying the
angle of approach to the same material**, not just the timetable. Basketball version:
massed = 100 free throws from the same spot; interleaved = five shots from here, five
from a step left, five with a defender, five under fatigue.

**How (cognitive angles for study):** Cover the same topic across sessions using
*different cognitive approaches*, roughly in ascending power:

1. Build structure/notes on the topic → days later, do practice questions on it (the
   common, solid combo).
2. Answer questions → then **write your own questions** (a genuinely different mental
   operation).
3. Write your own **model answer sheet** for your questions — different again, and one
   of the most effective moves available.
4. Exchange self-made exams with peers, answer theirs with no answer sheet, and discuss
   where your answers diverge from their model answers (see peer-testing).
5. Explain it, teach it, map it, apply it to a novel case, argue against it — each is
   another angle.

Interleaving and spacing work hand in hand: this session one or two angles, next
session different ones.

**Common mistakes:** Believing rotating subjects on a timer is the whole technique;
repeating the *same* method every review pass (rereading or rewriting notes again —
its benefit saturates on roughly the first pass, and every later pass costs the 10–20%
gain a different angle would have bought); treating interleaving as identical to spaced
repetition.

**Signs it's working:** Unfamiliar question formats stop being scary; you can enter
the topic from any point; mixed/curveball questions score as well as standard ones.
