# The learning log (lightweight, strategy-level)

A learning log is a **feedback tool, not notes**. Its only job: "what am I trying to
improve, what method did I use, and did it work?" It exists to stop random practice and
refine the learning system over time — the moment it becomes admin work it has failed.
Sung: you can only change 1–2 habits at a time, so improvement is a long series of
experiments, and unlogged experiments repeat themselves.

## The format (1–3 minutes per entry)

```
Subject/topic:  Maths — integration by substitution
Goal:           Solve medium questions independently
Strategy:       worked examples → closed-book practice
Result/gap:     Can solve once u is chosen; can't choose u reliably
Next move:      10 questions where I only identify u, no full solving
```

Rules that keep it lightweight:

- **One entry per meaningful study objective** (a "learning block"), never per fact or
  sub-topic. Three subjects in one session = one page with three small rows. A topic
  gets its own entry only when it's hard and keeps causing problems.
- **Before studying: one line** of intention ("today's target: X"). The real entry is
  the *after* reflection — what worked, what didn't, what changes next time.
- **Log strategies, not thinking moves.** "Brain dump + gap review" or "teach-back" go
  in the log; "I simplified, compared, connected…" does not — those are your default
  mental habits, running constantly, and logging them is noise (see
  levels-of-a-learning-system).

## When to change strategy (the bottleneck rule)

Do NOT rotate strategies for variety. **Keep a strategy while it's working; change it
when the bottleneck changes.**

Keep it if: you're improving, gaps are shrinking, the work still feels challenging, and
the strategy matches the output you need.

Modify it if: the same mistake repeats, you're bored/on autopilot, you can do it in one
context but not another, you understand but can't apply, or you can apply slowly but
not under pressure.

Switch it when **the type of gap changes** — and match the strategy to the gap:

| The gap | The right move |
|---|---|
| "I don't understand the concept" | concept encoding (deep processing loop, mapping) |
| "I understand but can't remember" | retrieval practice (free recall) |
| "I forgot the definition/fact" | flashcards |
| "I understand but can't explain why it matters" | teach-back, comparison, examples |
| "I understand the method but can't apply it" | practice problems |
| "I can solve isolated problems but fail mixed ones" | mixed/interleaved practice |
| "I can do it slowly but not in exam time" | timed practice |
| "I pass practice but not exam-style questions" | past-paper / output-aligned practice |

The example arc: worked examples help → they stop helping (you follow but can't solve)
→ closed-book practice → reveals algebra errors → targeted drills → algebra fine but
exam pressure kills you → timed mixed questions. Each change is bottleneck-driven, not
novelty-driven.

## Coaching cues

- Learner proposes an elaborate per-topic logging system → shrink it; the log serves
  study, it must never steal time from study.
- Learner switching techniques every session → bottleneck rule; what changed?
- Learner stuck for weeks with no log → start one; unlogged trial-and-error took Sung
  3 years that a log could have made 9 months.
- This app's technique-usage tracking (planned) is a built-in learning log — the coach
  should frame it that way.
