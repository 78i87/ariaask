# Peer testing (make exams for each other)

**What it's for:** Making study-with-friends actually productive. Two real benefits of
group study: (1) accountability and focus, and (2) — massively underrated — using each
other to find knowledge gaps.

**When:** Any group of 2–4 learners covering the same material, especially in exam prep.

**How:**
1. **Structure the accountability.** Group study only works when everyone shares the
   agenda ("we're here to keep each other focused and honest"). Use timers, a laid-out
   schedule, and assigned roles (timekeeper, distraction-monitor). Treat roles as jobs.
   Unstructured co-studying usually makes friends the distraction.
2. **Make practice exams for each other — from memory.** Each person writes a practice
   test on the shared material as part of their own spaced retrieval session. Writing
   good questions from memory is itself free recall plus high-level processing; checking
   your draft against notes afterwards and writing a model answer sheet is more.
3. **Exchange and sit each other's exams.** With 3–4 people, the time it takes to make
   one exam yields three more — an "infinite practice paper glitch."
4. **Exploit the missing answer sheet.** Answering a peer's exam with no official
   answers forces you to verify your own answers critically against the material. Where
   your answer differs from their model answer is gold: those differences usually mark
   deep, nuanced gaps — discuss them.

**Common mistakes:** Quizzing each other casually and calling it a system; making exams
open-book (kills the retrieval); groups without shared purpose or structure; only
writing questions you already know the answers to (write the hard ones — the exam will).

**Signs it's working:** Sessions end with a list of specific disagreements discussed;
each member's exams get harder over time; you look forward to it (it's genuinely fun).
